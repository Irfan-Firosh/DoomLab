The game waits between calls and every prompt already contains the current view, so the observe tool can never show anything new. Its description says so. The agents called it anyway: 663 of 3,369 decisions on the test levels, and about one in five on the dev levels too. Episodes that ran out of their call budget had spent a fifth of it here.

Finding 2 · the improvement loop

The lesson fixed the metric, not the game

An Analyst LLM read the counted failures and 11 corrected decisions and proposed three lessons for the single agent's prompt (policy v1). Offline it looked like a clear win. On five fresh validation levels it survived shorter on most of them: it stopped calling observe and started turning back and forth on the spot instead.

  1. Never call observe: the game is paused so it shows nothing new. Instead, turn to search for medikits or enemies.
  2. When health is below 50, walk to the nearest visible medikit immediately; do not turn away or look around.
  3. Aim at the nearest enemy before attacking, and fire only one shot (one hit kills). Never turn away from a visible enemy.
  • 3 → 7of 11 corrected decisions answered correctly (asked 3 times each).
  • 4 of 5validation levels where the new policy survived for less time.

Inside one episode

The commander switching jobs

The commander team on the same level: 24 mode choices, 23 switches between the Combat specialist (aim, shoot) and the Navigator (turn to look around), 24 kills, then its health ran out at tic 1,413. The router only asks the commander again when the situation changes; in between, the current specialist keeps going without an extra call.

How it works

One harness, four players, every step on record

  1. PerceivevizDoom state becomes facts: what is visible, where, how far, health and ammo.
  2. DecideAn agent picks one typed tool call: random, hand-written rules, one Qwen agent, or a Qwen commander with two specialists.
  3. Validate and actOne executor checks every call and is the only code that presses buttons. Bad calls are rejected with a reason.
  4. Route and recoverA small state graph decides what happens next; three rejections in a row end the agent's turn.
  5. RecordEvery step goes to a trace that replays the game exactly, frame for frame.
  6. EvaluateSeeds split into dev, validation and a locked test set; scores come from traces only.
  7. ImproveMined failures and reviewed corrections become proposed lessons, tested and gated, then a human decides.

Caveats

  • One model (qwen3.8:27b through Purdue GenAI Studio), three vizDoom scenarios, 10 test levels per cell: the 95% intervals are wide and most differences are pilot-sized.
  • Scores on defend_the_center are survival within an equal budget of 150 LLM calls; both LLM agents ran out on some levels and then stood still.
  • Of the 11 corrections behind the improvement trial, 2 were written by a human reviewer and 9 were drafted by an AI assistant from the ViZDoom scenario docs; they are labelled as such.
  • A hosted model is not fully repeatable at temperature 0; regression cases are asked three times for that reason.
